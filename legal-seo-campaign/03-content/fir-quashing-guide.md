# How FIR Quashing Works in Bombay High Court: A Complete Guide (2026)

*Last Updated: May 2026 | Jurisdiction: Maharashtra | Applicable Law: BNSS 2023, Section 528*

---

## Introduction

An FIR — First Information Report — is the document that sets the criminal justice machinery in motion. Once an FIR is filed, the police have an obligation to investigate. The consequences for the named accused can be immediate and severe: potential arrest, social stigma, professional damage, and family disruption.

But what if the FIR is false? What if it discloses no criminal offence? What if it is an abuse of the legal process?

In these situations, Indian law provides a remedy: **FIR quashing**. This guide explains how FIR quashing works in Maharashtra, specifically before the Bombay High Court, under the current legal framework of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) 2023.

---

## What Is FIR Quashing? Understanding Section 528 BNSS

**FIR quashing** is the legal process by which a High Court exercises its inherent jurisdiction to set aside an FIR where allowing the matter to continue would amount to an abuse of the court's process or a miscarriage of justice.

Under the **BNSS 2023, Section 528** preserves the High Court's inherent powers, replacing the former **Section 482 of the CrPC 1973**. The court is empowered to:
- Give effect to any order under BNSS
- Prevent abuse of the process of any court
- Secure the ends of justice

*Note: Section 528 BNSS is the correct provision to cite in quashing petitions filed after July 1, 2024. For matters where the FIR was registered before that date, courts typically refer to both old and new provisions.*

---

## Grounds for FIR Quashing

The Supreme Court's landmark case *State of Haryana v. Bhajan Lal* (1992) remains the foundational authority. Established grounds include:

### Ground 1: Allegations Do Not Constitute a Cognisable Offence
If the FIR, read on its face, does not disclose ingredients of any criminal offence — even accepting every allegation as true — it cannot survive.

**Example:** A property dispute where a civil breach of contract is described in criminal language does not automatically become a cognisable offence.

### Ground 2: Allegations Are Patently Absurd
Where the allegations, even taken at face value, are so inherently incredible or contradictory that no reasonable person could believe them.

### Ground 3: No Sufficient Ground for Proceeding
The court examines whether the allegations, even if true, justify criminal prosecution.

### Ground 4: FIR Is Filed to Settle Personal Scores or Civil Disputes
Courts have repeatedly held that criminal law cannot become a tool for harassment in matrimonial disputes, partnership breakdowns, or property conflicts.

### Ground 5: Settled Matters (Compounding Between Parties)
Where the dispute is between private parties and the parties have reached a genuine settlement, the High Court can quash the FIR in the interest of doing complete justice (per *Gian Singh v. State of Punjab*, 2012, and *Narinder Singh* cases).

### Ground 6: Violation of Fundamental Rights
Where the criminal proceeding itself violates the accused's constitutional rights.

## When FIR Quashing Is NOT Possible

- **Serious offences involving public interest:** Murder, rape, offences under POCSO, NDPS commercial quantity, organised crime, UAPA
- **Where investigation is incomplete and genuine**
- **Where factual disputes require trial**
- **Where settlement is not genuine**
- **Chargesheet filed and cognisance taken in serious matters**

## Bombay High Court: Specific Process

### Step 1: Filing the Petition
A quashing petition is filed as a **Criminal Miscellaneous Application** invoking the court's inherent jurisdiction under Section 528 BNSS. The petition must:
- Identify the FIR (police station, FIR number, year, offences alleged)
- Set out the facts of the case
- State the specific grounds for quashing with legal support
- Annex settlement agreement and affidavit of complainant (where relevant)

### Step 2: Listing and Interim Stay
A critical relief is the **interim stay on investigation** — an order directing the police to stay the investigation while the quashing petition is being heard. This prevents arrest and stops the investigation in its tracks.

### Step 3: Notice to State and Complainant
The court issues notice to:
- The State of Maharashtra (through the Public Prosecutor)
- The original complainant (in private party cases)

### Step 4: Arguments and Final Hearing
After replies are filed, the matter is listed for final arguments.

### Step 5: Order
The Bombay High Court either:
- **Quashes the FIR/proceedings** — the case ends
- **Refuses quashing** — the case proceeds; interim stay is vacated
- **Disposes with directions** — gives directions about the manner of investigation

## Documents Required

- Copy of the FIR
- Copy of any complaint / complaint application that preceded the FIR
- Copy of all notices / summons received by the accused
- Copy of chargesheet (if filed)
- Any orders passed by lower courts in the matter
- Identity documents of the petitioner
- In settlement matters: notarised settlement agreement and affidavit of complainant
- Documents supporting the grounds of quashing

---

## Interim Stay on Investigation: What It Means

When the Bombay High Court grants an interim stay on investigation:
- The police cannot arrest the petitioner in connection with the FIR
- Further investigative steps like searches, seizures, or custodial interrogation are stayed
- Chargesheet cannot be filed while the stay is in force

*The interim stay is not automatic. It must be argued and obtained.*

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Waiting for the Chargesheet Before Filing** — The High Court's quashing jurisdiction applies at any stage, including very early in the investigation.

**Mistake 2: Filing Without a Genuine Legal Basis** — Have an experienced criminal advocate assess whether genuine quashing grounds exist before filing.

**Mistake 3: Settlement Not Properly Documented** — Courts insist on proper notarised settlement deeds and complainant affidavits.

**Mistake 4: Not Appearing Before Lower Court While Quashing is Pending** — A quashing petition at the HC does not automatically stay proceedings in the trial/lower court.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can an FIR be quashed after the chargesheet has been filed?
Yes. The Bombay High Court has jurisdiction to quash criminal proceedings even after the chargesheet has been filed. However, courts are generally more cautious at an advanced stage in serious matters.

### 2. Can an FIR be quashed on compromise in a non-compoundable offence?
For offences primarily private in nature — even if technically non-compoundable — a genuine settlement between the parties can be a basis for quashing (per *Narinder Singh v. State of Punjab*, 2014). This does NOT apply to serious offences involving public harm.

### 3. If my FIR quashing petition is rejected, what options do I have?
- Filing a Special Leave Petition (SLP) before the Supreme Court
- Contesting the matter before the trial court on merits
- Pursuing anticipatory bail or regular bail

### 4. Can the complainant withdraw an FIR?
Once an FIR for a cognisable offence is registered, the complainant alone cannot withdraw it — the investigation becomes a State matter. However, where the complainant executes a proper affidavit withdrawing support, courts view this positively in quashing petitions.

### 5. How is a cyber crime FIR quashing case different?
Cyber crime FIRs require careful analysis of:
- Whether the alleged acts constitute a criminal offence or a civil dispute
- Whether the IT Act provisions cited are correctly invoked
- The role of digital evidence

### 6. Does the Bombay High Court accept video conferencing for quashing hearings?
Yes. For preliminary hearings and routine adjournments, video conferencing is available. For final arguments, physical presence is typically preferred and more effective.
