## Emergency FIR & Arrest Response Checklist
### For Persons Facing Police Action, FIR, or Arrest Threat in Maharashtra

*Prepared for general informational purposes by Advocate Akash Chikate, Pune. This checklist is educational and does not constitute legal advice.*

---

## CATEGORY 1: IMMEDIATE ACTIONS (0–2 HOURS)

- [ ] **1. Do not panic — pause before responding**

  Your first reaction matters. Do not immediately call the complainant, message anyone about the case, or visit the police station alone. Panic-driven decisions in the first two hours are among the most common sources of later problems.

- [ ] **2. Do not speak to the police without legal advice**

  You have the right under Article 20(3) of the Constitution of India not to be compelled to be a witness against yourself. Until you have spoken to a lawyer, do not give any statement — written or verbal — to police.

- [ ] **3. Contact a criminal lawyer immediately**

  This is the single most important step. A criminal advocate can advise on whether you need anticipatory bail, how to respond to summons, and what legal strategy is appropriate. Do not wait until you are arrested.

  *Advocate Akash Chikate's contact: advocateakashchikate.com/contact*

- [ ] **4. Secure a copy of the FIR if possible**

  You have the legal right to access a copy of the FIR. FIRs are available online through the Maharashtra Police website (citizen.mahapolice.gov.in).

- [ ] **5. Do not leave Maharashtra or India without legal advice**

  Leaving the state or country could be viewed negatively by courts and may violate conditions of any bail or protection you subsequently receive.

- [ ] **6. Preserve all relevant digital evidence**

  Do not delete messages, emails, call recordings, photographs, or any other digital material. Deletion of evidence can itself become an issue.

- [ ] **7. Do not contact the complainant or witnesses**

  Contacting the complainant or potential witnesses after an FIR is registered can be construed as tampering with evidence.

- [ ] **8. Brief your immediate family**

  Family members should be instructed not to speak to police without the family's advocate present.

---

## CATEGORY 2: FIRST 24 HOURS

- [ ] **9. Provide your advocate a complete and accurate account**

  Attorney-client privilege protects everything you say to your advocate. Do not withhold facts — even embarrassing ones. Full disclosure is essential.

- [ ] **10. Do not discuss the case on social media or with friends/colleagues**

  Social media posts about your case can be screenshot and used against you. Implement complete social media silence.

- [ ] **11. Identify and brief your surety**

  If anticipatory bail is to be sought, identify a surety (family member, friend, colleague who will stand as guarantor) and gather their identity proof, address proof, and property details.

- [ ] **12. Gather your identity documents**

  Compile: Aadhaar card, PAN card, passport, voter ID. At least two forms of current valid ID.

- [ ] **13. Note down a clear timeline of events**

  Write a clear chronological account of your relationship with the complainant and the events that led to the FIR. This memo is invaluable for your advocate.

- [ ] **14. If police visit your home or office: ask to see the warrant**

  You are entitled to ask to see the warrant. Do not physically resist police action, but do exercise your right to know the basis and immediately contact your advocate.

- [ ] **15. If you are arrested: assert your right to legal counsel immediately**

  Upon arrest, you have the right under Section 303 BNSS and Article 22 of the Constitution to be represented by an advocate of your choice. Do not give any statement to police before speaking to your lawyer.

---

## CATEGORY 3: WITHIN 3 DAYS

- [ ] **16. Confirm your advocate has filed or is filing the bail application**

  Confirm the application has been prepared and is being filed. Understand the court date and what is expected to happen at the first hearing.

- [ ] **17. Arrange for surety to be present at court**

  Your surety will need to be present when bail is actually executed.

- [ ] **18. Comply strictly with any interim court order**

  Read every word of the order with your advocate. Strictly comply. Non-compliance can result in cancellation of bail.

- [ ] **19. Keep a dedicated communication channel with your advocate**

  Establish a clear, responsive channel. Criminal matters move quickly.

- [ ] **20. Suspend any related business or financial transactions**

  If the FIR involves financial fraud allegations, consult your advocate before making any further transactions mentioned in the FIR.

---

## CATEGORY 4: LEGAL DOCUMENTATION CHECKLIST

- [ ] **21. Copy of FIR** — from police station or online portal
- [ ] **22. All police notices, summons, letters** — every written communication from any police station
- [ ] **23. Any previous complaints** filed by you or by the complainant against you
- [ ] **24. All written communication with the complainant** — emails, WhatsApp messages, letters, contracts
- [ ] **25. Financial documents** — if the case involves financial disputes
- [ ] **26. Surety documents** — surety's Aadhaar, PAN, address proof, property documents
- [ ] **27. Proof of residence and domicile** — to establish you are rooted in the community
- [ ] **28. Employment / business proof** — offer letter, service certificate, business registration
- [ ] **29. Any prior court orders** in connected matters
- [ ] **30. Medical records** — if health condition is relevant
