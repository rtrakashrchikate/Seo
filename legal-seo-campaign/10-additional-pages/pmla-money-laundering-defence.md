The Prevention of Money Laundering Act 2002 (PMLA) gives the Enforcement Directorate (ED) sweeping powers to investigate, arrest, attach property, and prosecute. A PMLA case often begins with an ED notice or raid — and the consequences move quickly: bank accounts frozen, property attached, and arrest without the procedural protections available in ordinary criminal matters.

## What is Money Laundering Under PMLA?

Under Section 3 of the PMLA, money laundering occurs when a person:
- Directly or indirectly attempts to indulge in, or assists, or is a party to, or is actually involved in
- Any process or activity connected with the **proceeds of crime**
- Including concealment, possession, acquisition, use, projection as untainted, or claiming as untainted property

**"Proceeds of crime"** means any property derived or obtained directly or indirectly as a result of any criminal activity relating to a **Scheduled Offence** — i.e., specific offences listed in the PMLA Schedule (which includes fraud, corruption, drug trafficking, terror financing, and more).

**Punishment under PMLA Section 4:** Rigorous imprisonment of **3 to 7 years**, extendable to 10 years where the Scheduled Offence is under NDPS Act.

## The ED Investigation Process

**Stage 1: Predicate Offence FIR**
PMLA investigation always traces back to a predicate (underlying) offence — typically an FIR under BNS for cheating, fraud, corruption, NDPS, etc. Once ED registers an Enforcement Case Information Report (ECIR), it runs parallel to the police investigation.

**Stage 2: Summons / Notice Under Section 50 PMLA**
ED issues summons requiring appearance for statement recording. These are **not optional** — failure to appear can itself lead to arrest. Statements recorded under Section 50 are admissible as evidence (unlike police statements under CrPC/BNSS).

**Stage 3: Search and Seizure**
ED can conduct searches and seize documents, digital devices, cash, and property under Section 17 PMLA.

**Stage 4: Provisional Attachment**
ED can provisionally attach property (bank accounts, real estate, vehicles) under Section 5 PMLA if it believes the property constitutes proceeds of crime. Attachment can occur even before arrest.

**Stage 5: Arrest**
ED can arrest under Section 19 PMLA if it has reason to believe the person is guilty of money laundering.

**Stage 6: Adjudicating Authority**
The Enforcement Directorate's attachment is confirmed or rejected by the Adjudicating Authority (PMLA). Appeals go to the Appellate Tribunal.

**Stage 7: Special Court (PMLA)**
Criminal prosecution under PMLA is before Special Courts constituted under Section 43 PMLA.

## PMLA Bail — Why It Is Difficult

Section 45 PMLA imposes a **twin condition bail test** similar to NDPS Section 37 but broader:

Before granting bail in PMLA, the court must be satisfied that:
1. There are **reasonable grounds for believing the accused is not guilty** of the offence, AND
2. The accused is **not likely to commit any offence** while on bail

Both conditions must be satisfied simultaneously. Courts interpret Section 45 strictly. Bail in PMLA cases is genuinely difficult and requires strong grounds, well-prepared applications, and effective legal argument.

**Grounds that have succeeded in PMLA bail:**
- Prosecution failing to demonstrate proceeds of crime traceable to the accused specifically
- Predicate offence itself is weak or being challenged
- Accused has cooperated with investigation
- Accused is the beneficiary of a corporate transaction, not the architect of fraud
- Long custody without meaningful trial progress
- Medical grounds

## Challenging Property Attachment

ED's provisional attachment under Section 5 PMLA can be challenged before the Adjudicating Authority and the PMLA Appellate Tribunal. Grounds include:

- Property does not constitute proceeds of crime
- Property was acquired from legitimate sources prior to any alleged offence
- The predicate offence itself is under challenge or has been quashed
- Property belongs to a bona fide third party with no nexus to the alleged offence
- Procedural non-compliance in attachment order

## Defence Strategies in PMLA Cases

**1. Attack the Predicate Offence**
PMLA cannot stand if the predicate (Scheduled) offence is quashed, discharged, or acquitted. If the underlying FIR is successfully challenged under Section 528 BNSS, the PMLA proceeding is significantly weakened.

**2. No Proceeds of Crime**
The prosecution must establish that the accused received or dealt with "proceeds of crime" — money or property directly traceable to the Scheduled Offence. Where the money trail is absent, broken, or demonstrates legitimate transactions, the PMLA case weakens.

**3. Section 50 Statement Challenges**
Statements recorded by ED under Section 50 PMLA are admissible as evidence. Defence examines whether the statement was voluntarily made, properly recorded, and not coerced.

**4. Double Jeopardy / Separate Offences**
PMLA prosecution runs alongside — not instead of — the predicate offence prosecution. Both are separate. The accused faces two separate trials. This creates procedural complexity that experienced defence counsel navigates.

**5. KYC and Documentary Paper Trail**
In many PMLA cases, the accused can demonstrate that transactions were properly documented, bank-certified, and tax-compliant. A clean paper trail is one of the strongest defences.

## Key Differences: ED vs. Police Investigation

| Aspect | Police (BNSS) | ED (PMLA) |
|--------|--------------|----------|
| Statement admissibility | Not admissible as evidence | Admissible as evidence (Section 50) |
| Bail standard | Standard BNSS factors | Twin conditions (Section 45) |
| Property attachment | Court freezes (CrPC 102) | ED provisional attachment (Section 5) |
| Chargesheet deadline | 60/90 days | No fixed statutory deadline |
| Remand | Police/judicial remand | ED custody remand |
| Information sharing | FIR public | ECIR not public |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: I received an ED summons under PMLA. Must I appear?**
Yes. Summons under Section 50 PMLA must be complied with. Failure to appear without reasonable cause can lead to ED arrest. Before appearing, consult a criminal defence lawyer. You have the right to have counsel present during questioning.

**Q: Can ED arrest without a court warrant?**
Yes. Under Section 19 PMLA, the ED Director, Deputy Director, or Assistant Director can arrest a person if there are reasons to believe they are guilty of money laundering. No court warrant is required for the arrest itself.

**Q: My bank account has been frozen by ED. What can I do?**
ED provisional attachment of bank accounts under Section 5 PMLA must be confirmed by the Adjudicating Authority within 60 days. You can challenge the attachment before the Adjudicating Authority and, if unsuccessful, before the PMLA Appellate Tribunal and the Bombay High Court.

**Q: How is PMLA different from regular fraud cases?**
In a regular fraud case (BNS 318), police investigate and file a chargesheet. In PMLA, ED simultaneously investigates the money trail — how alleged fraud proceeds moved, who received them, and where they are now. The ED can attach property globally, freeze accounts, and arrest independently of police. Both investigations run in parallel.

**Q: Is PMLA bail possible?**
PMLA bail under Section 45 is subject to twin conditions — the court must be satisfied that the accused appears not guilty and will not reoffend. It is more difficult than standard bail but has been granted in cases with weak evidence, long custody, or strong defence grounds.
