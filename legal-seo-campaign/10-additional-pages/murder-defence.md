**All offences under BNS 101-109 are non-bailable and cognisable.** Police can arrest without a warrant and bail requires a court order.

## The Critical Distinction: Murder vs. Culpable Homicide

The most important legal distinction in homicide cases is between **murder** (BNS 101) and **culpable homicide not amounting to murder** (BNS 100). This distinction determines the punishment:

**Murder (BNS 101)** requires:
- Intention to cause death, OR
- Intention to cause bodily injury likely to cause death, OR
- Knowledge that the act is imminently dangerous and likely to cause death

**Culpable homicide not amounting to murder** applies where:
- The act was done under grave and sudden provocation
- The accused exceeded the right of private defence
- The accused acted in the heat of passion without premeditation
- The knowledge of causing death was present but not the intention

This distinction — which can mean the difference between the death penalty and 10 years imprisonment — is what skilled defence counsel argues in every homicide case. The specific facts, the nature of the weapon, the number of injuries, the body parts targeted, and the circumstances all bear on which provision applies.

## Bail in Murder Cases

Murder under BNS 101 is non-bailable. Bail must be sought from:
- **Sessions Court** — first application
- **Bombay High Court** — if Sessions Court rejects, or for matters of complexity

**Factors courts consider in murder bail:**
- Prima facie strength of prosecution case
- Nature of the evidence (eyewitness, circumstantial, forensic)
- Criminal antecedents of the accused
- Risk of absconding
- Period of custody already undergone
- Whether trial is proceeding or delayed
- Role of the specific accused (main accused vs. co-accused)

Bail in murder cases is genuinely difficult but not impossible. Long pre-trial custody, delay in trial, weak forensic evidence, or the specific limited role of the accused are grounds courts have considered.

**Anticipatory bail** is occasionally applied for in cases where a person apprehends arrest in connection with a homicide that has not yet been registered as murder — for example, where someone has died in disputed circumstances and an FIR under BNS 100/101 is anticipated.

## Key Defence Strategies in Homicide Cases

**1. Right of Private Defence (BNS 34-44)**
Where death occurred while the accused was defending themselves or another person from an assault, the right of private defence is a complete defence. The law allows force proportionate to the threat faced. Where the force used was excessive, the offence may be reduced to culpable homicide under BNS 104.

**2. Sudden Grave Provocation**
Where the act was committed in the heat of passion following sudden grave provocation, without premeditation and without time for the passion to cool, the offence is reduced from murder to culpable homicide not amounting to murder.

**3. Challenging Eyewitness Testimony**
In many murder cases, eyewitness testimony is the primary evidence. Defence examines: whether the witness had clear visibility, the lighting conditions, the distance from the incident, whether there is a motive to falsely identify the accused, and whether the identification was properly conducted (Test Identification Parade).

**4. Forensic Evidence Challenges**
Forensic evidence including post-mortem reports, ballistic reports, blood grouping, DNA, and fingerprint analysis can be challenged on: methodology of collection, chain of custody, qualification of the forensic expert, and consistency with the prosecution's case theory.

**5. Alibi**
Where the accused was provably elsewhere at the time of the incident, alibi evidence supported by CCTV footage, digital location data, ticket records, or credible witness testimony provides a complete defence.

**6. Circumstantial Evidence — Last Seen Theory**
Many murder cases rely on circumstantial evidence including the "last seen" theory. Courts require that the chain of circumstances is complete and consistent only with the guilt of the accused. Defence identifies any gaps in the chain.

**7. Co-Accused — Specific Role**
In cases with multiple accused, defence examines whether the specific client had the common intention required under BNS 3(5) (equivalent to Section 34 IPC). Liability does not attach to every co-accused automatically.

## Procedure — Murder Trial in Pune

1. **FIR** registered at local police station
2. **Investigation** — panchanama, spot inspection, post-mortem, forensic samples
3. **Chargesheet** filed before Judicial Magistrate within 60/90 days
4. **Committal** to Sessions Court (murder is exclusively triable by Sessions Court)
5. **Charges framed** by Sessions Court
6. **Prosecution evidence** — examination of witnesses, documents, forensic reports
7. **Defence evidence** — accused's statement under Section 351 BNSS, defence witnesses
8. **Arguments** by prosecution and defence
9. **Judgment** — conviction or acquittal
10. **Appeal** to Bombay High Court if convicted

Murder trials in Pune Sessions Court typically take 3–7 years from chargesheet to judgment, depending on the number of witnesses and complexity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can bail be obtained in a murder case in Pune?**
Bail in murder cases (BNS 101) is not a matter of right. It must be argued before the Sessions Court and if refused, before the Bombay High Court. Grounds include: weak prosecution evidence, long pre-trial custody, the accused's specific limited role in the incident, and delay in trial. It is difficult but not impossible.

**Q: What is the difference between murder and attempt to murder?**
Murder (BNS 101) requires that death has actually occurred. Attempt to murder (BNS 108) applies where the accused, with intent to cause death or knowledge that the act is likely to cause death, does an act that falls short of causing death. Attempt to murder carries up to 10 years imprisonment, and up to life if hurt is actually caused.

**Q: What is culpable homicide not amounting to murder?**
Culpable homicide not amounting to murder (BNS 100) is a less serious form of homicide. It applies where death was caused without full murderous intent — for example, under sudden grave provocation, or where the accused exceeded the right of private defence. The punishment is up to 10 years or life imprisonment, significantly less than murder.

**Q: Is the death penalty given in murder cases in Maharashtra?**
The death penalty is the maximum punishment for murder under BNS 101 but is imposed only in the "rarest of rare" cases — those involving exceptional brutality, multiple victims, or heinous circumstances. Most murder convictions result in life imprisonment. Both the Sessions Court (for conviction) and the Bombay High Court (for death sentence confirmation) must agree before execution.

**Q: What should I do if I am accused of murder?**
Contact a criminal defence lawyer immediately. Do not make any statement to police without counsel present. Anything said to police can be used against you in trial. If not yet arrested, consider anticipatory bail. Preserve any evidence that supports your account of events.
