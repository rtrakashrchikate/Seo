All offences under BNS 63–70 are **non-bailable and cognisable**. This means police can arrest without a warrant and bail is not a matter of right — it must be argued and granted by a court.

## Bail in Sexual Assault Cases

Because these offences are non-bailable, bail requires a formal application before a Judicial Magistrate First Class (JMFC) or Sessions Court. Courts consider:

- Nature and gravity of the accusation
- Antecedents of the accused (prior criminal record)
- Risk of the accused absconding
- Risk of tampering with evidence or influencing witnesses
- Medical evidence on record
- Prior relationship between accused and complainant

**Anticipatory bail** is available under Section 482 BNSS before arrest in cases where a complaint has been filed but no arrest has been made. This is particularly relevant where the complaint arises out of a consensual relationship, a matrimonial or property dispute, or other circumstances where the criminal complaint may be contested.

## Common Defence Arguments in Sexual Assault Cases

Every case has its own facts. Common grounds that defence counsel examines include:

**1. Consent**
Where the allegation arises from a consensual relationship, the defence examines communications (messages, call records), prior meetings, photographs, and the nature of the relationship. BNS Section 63 specifically defines "consent" — courts have held that consent freely given and understood negates the offence.

**2. Promise to Marry Disputes (BNS 69)**
BNS 69 criminalises sexual intercourse obtained by deceitful promise of marriage. Courts have held that a bona fide promise that later could not be fulfilled due to circumstances does not automatically constitute the offence — the prosecution must prove the promise was false from the inception.

**3. Delay in Filing FIR**
An unexplained delay of weeks or months in filing the FIR after the alleged incident is a factor courts weigh. While delay alone does not prove falsity, unexplained delay requires explanation by the prosecution.

**4. Medical Evidence**
The prosecution must establish medical evidence consistent with the allegation. Where medical records are inconsistent with the complainant's account, this is a significant defence point.

**5. Contradictions in Statement**
The complainant's statement before the Magistrate (Section 164 BNSS statement), the FIR, and trial evidence are examined for material contradictions. Contradictions on material facts go to the credibility of the allegation.

**6. Motive to Falsely Implicate**
In cases arising from failed relationships, property disputes, matrimonial discord, or personal enmity, the defence examines whether there is a demonstrable motive for a false complaint.

**7. Alibi**
Where the accused was provably in a different location at the time of the alleged incident, alibi evidence supported by documentary proof (CCTV, digital records, witness testimony) constitutes a complete defence.

## Special Court Procedure in Pune

Rape trials in Maharashtra are conducted in **Special Courts designated under the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act 2013**. Key procedural points:

- Trial must be completed within **2 months** (target under law — in practice longer)
- In-camera proceedings (not open court) to protect the complainant's identity
- Complainant's identity cannot be disclosed publicly
- Section 53A BNS: character evidence of the complainant regarding past sexual history is inadmissible
- Video conferencing of complainant's evidence is possible
- Defence counsel cannot directly cross-examine the complainant in POCSO matters — questions are routed through the court

## Digital Evidence in Sexual Assault Cases

Under BPSS 2023 (Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam), digital evidence — WhatsApp messages, photographs, call records, social media — plays a significant role. Defence examines:

- Authenticity of digital evidence (Section 63 BPSS certification requirements)
- Whether messages are presented in complete context or selectively excerpted
- Chain of custody of electronic devices
- Whether digital forensics was conducted properly

## Protecting Your Rights — Immediate Steps

If you are accused or anticipate a complaint:

1. **Do not make any statement to police without legal counsel present**
2. **Contact a criminal defence lawyer immediately** — before any arrest if possible

5. **Consider anticipatory bail** if arrest is anticipated — Section 482 BNSS provides this relief before arrest

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is bail available in rape cases in India?**
Bail in rape cases (BNS 63 and related sections) is not a matter of right — it must be argued before a Sessions Court or Bombay High Court. The court applies factors including gravity of the offence, risk of absconding, and risk of tampering with evidence. Bail is possible but requires strong grounds and effective legal representation.

**Q: What is the difference between BNS 63 and BNS 69?**
BNS 63 covers rape in all its forms. BNS 69 specifically covers sexual intercourse by a man with a woman by deceitful means including a false promise of marriage or employment. Courts have held that BNS 69 requires proof that the promise was false from the beginning — a promise that broke down later for genuine reasons may not constitute this offence.

**Q: Can anticipatory bail be obtained in a rape case?**
Anticipatory bail under Section 482 BNSS can be applied for before arrest. Courts apply a higher standard of scrutiny in sexual offence matters. Whether anticipatory bail is granted depends on the specific facts, the grounds presented, and the court's assessment of the material on record.

**Q: Will my name be published if I am accused?**
The law prohibits publication of the complainant's identity. There is no similar prohibition on publication of the accused's identity. However, accused persons who are acquitted have sought remedies for reputation damage. Your lawyer can advise on what steps, if any, are available.

**Q: How long does a rape trial take in Pune?**
While the law mandates completion within 2 months, in practice rape trials at Pune Special Courts take significantly longer due to caseload. Appeals to the Bombay High Court can take additional years.

**Q: What is the role of defence counsel in a rape trial?**
Defence counsel ensures the accused receives a fair trial, that the prosecution proves its case beyond reasonable doubt, that evidence is properly admitted under BPSS 2023, that witnesses are properly cross-examined, and that the accused's constitutional rights are protected throughout.
